# Art Pepper Meets The Rhythm Section on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Art Pepper Meets The Rhythm Section* was recorded in a single day, January 19, 1957, at Contemporary's studio in Los Angeles, produced by label founder Lester Koenig and engineered by Roy DuNann. The album's premise is right there in the title: West Coast altoist Art Pepper paired with the touring rhythm section of Miles Davis's first great quintet – Red Garland on piano, Paul Chambers on bass, and Philly Joe Jones on drums – a genuine East-meets-West summit session rather than a marketing conceit. What makes the record's reputation even more remarkable is the circumstances behind it. By Pepper's own account, he only learned about the session the morning he was due in the studio, hadn't met any of the other musicians beforehand despite admiring all three, was struggling with a drug problem, and was playing a saxophone in poor repair. Sources disagree on exactly how long he'd gone without playing before the date – Koenig's original liner notes say two weeks, Pepper's autobiography *Straight Life* says six months, though the book's own discography suggests he'd actually recorded several sessions in the preceding weeks, including one just five days earlier. Whatever the truth of his preparation, the results are widely considered among the best playing of his career, and the album is regularly cited as one of the most important records in jazz history.

Contemporary released it in July 1957 as Contemporary C3532 (mono), with a stereo edition following in 1958. The stereo catalog history is itself a small piece of trivia: sources disagree on whether the first stereo issue carried the number S7008 on Contemporary's short-lived "Stereo Records" sub-imprint or S7532 on Contemporary's own numbering – both plausibly existed at different points, since several labels of this era briefly used a separate stereo imprint before folding stereo releases into their main numbering scheme. The original cover photograph, taken by William Claxton in a small grove of trees off Fargo Street in Los Angeles near where Pepper lived at the time, was reused on at least one other Pepper release.

Collectors have a genuine, still-unresolved debate about the earliest pressing variant: original copies of C3532 exist with both red and blue cover designs, and which one came first is a matter of real disagreement rather than settled fact – LondonJazzCollector's detailed writeup on the subject leans toward red as the likelier original based on circumstantial pricing evidence, but treats it as exactly that, circumstantial, not proven. A later "green title and box" variant is generally not considered an original first pressing at all.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Contemporary C3532](https://www.discogs.com/release/2993318) – 1957 original mono pressing; see the note above on the unresolved red/blue cover debate.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** Analogue Productions UHQR 0022-45 (2025) – the label's top-tier Ultra High Quality Record treatment, see below.

**Best-value modern:** [Original Jazz Classics OJC-338](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Pepper_Meets_the_Rhythm_Section) – standard Fantasy-era reissue; no standalone Discogs release ID confirmed in this pass.

## Analogue Productions' three distinct reissues of this title

This is one of the most heavily audiophile-reissued titles in the Contemporary catalog, and it's worth being precise about which Analogue Productions edition is which, since the label has returned to it multiple times over three decades. The earliest was APJ 010 (1992), a standard 33⅓ pressing. In 2003, Analogue Productions issued AJAZ 7532, a 2×45rpm 180-gram edition mastered from the original tapes – this is the pressing Discogs owner reviews describe as "one of the great releases from the 45rpm series" and "best possible sound." Most recently, in August 2025, Analogue Productions issued a UHQR (Ultra High Quality Record) edition – catalog UHQR 0022-45 – on 200-gram Clarity Vinyl, mastered AAA by Bernie Grundman from the original analog tapes and pressed at Quality Record Pressings, limited to 2,750 numbered copies in a deluxe clamshell box with a 16-page booklet featuring new liner notes by John Koenig, son of Contemporary's founder. UHQR is Analogue Productions' flagship product line, generally considered a step above their standard 45rpm audiophile series, so this is very likely the current sonic reference for the title, though it's also the most expensive and hardest to find once the numbered run sells out.

Separately, Contemporary's original engineering has also been reissued more affordably through Craft Recordings' Acoustic Sounds Series (catalog CR00382), part of a 70th-anniversary Contemporary Records reissue program, also mastered by Bernie Grundman and pressed at Quality Record Pressings in a Stoughton tip-on jacket – a genuinely different, more accessible product from the UHQR despite sharing a mastering engineer. A separate Craft Recordings Record Store Day mono reissue (180-gram) also circulated around 2022–2023; sourcing on its exact release year and mastering credit wasn't confirmed with the same confidence as the other pressings in this guide, so it's mentioned here as a lead rather than included in the tier table below.

## Sound quality by pressing

### Contemporary C3532 (1957 original)
The true first pressing, though which cover color came first – red or blue – remains genuinely disputed among collectors rather than settled. Owner reviews of original copies describe the recording as remarkably dynamic and "live sounding" even decades later, with Roy DuNann's engineering singled out for how present the drums and bass feel in the mix.

### Analogue Productions UHQR 0022-45 (2025)
The current flagship reissue. 200-gram Clarity Vinyl pressed at Quality Record Pressings, cut at 45rpm across four sides by Bernie Grundman from the original analog tapes, packaged in a deluxe clamshell box. Owner reviews describe it as extremely dynamic and tonally accurate, with several noting that even modest turntable and cartridge setups can extract exceptional results from the wide-groove 45rpm cut.

### Analogue Productions AJAZ 7532 (2003, 45rpm)
The prior-generation Analogue Productions 45rpm edition, on standard 180-gram vinyl rather than the 2025 UHQR's 200-gram Clarity formulation. Reviewed enthusiastically at the time of release and still well-regarded, though the 2025 UHQR edition likely supersedes it as the current reference given Analogue Productions' own internal hierarchy between standard 45rpm and UHQR product lines.

### Craft Recordings Acoustic Sounds Series (CR00382)
A more accessible modern reissue than the UHQR, also mastered by Bernie Grundman and pressed at Quality Record Pressings, part of Craft's wider 70th-anniversary Contemporary Records reissue program.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$:** The Original Jazz Classics OJC-338 reissue – standard, easy-to-find, and affordable.

**$$$:** The Craft Recordings Acoustic Sounds Series edition (CR00382) – Bernie Grundman mastering at a more accessible price than the UHQR.

**$$$$:** A clean [Contemporary C3532](https://www.discogs.com/release/2993318) original, condition-dependent – note the unresolved red/blue first-cover debate before paying a premium for either color specifically.

**$$$$$:** The Analogue Productions UHQR 0022-45 (2025) – the label's top-tier treatment of this title, or the earlier AJAZ 7532 (2003) 45rpm edition if the UHQR proves hard to source.
