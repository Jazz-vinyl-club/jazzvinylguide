# Lady in Satin on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Lady in Satin* was recorded February 19–21, 1958 at Columbia's 30th Street Studio in New York City, produced by Irving Townsend and engineered by Fred Plaut. Ray Ellis conducted a roughly 40-piece orchestra behind Billie Holiday, a departure from the small-combo settings of most of her catalogue. Released June 1958 as Columbia CL 1157 (mono) and CS 8048 (stereo), it was Holiday's return to Columbia after sixteen years away, and the last album she completed and saw released before her death in July 1959. The mono LP includes one track, "The End of a Love Affair," not present on the original stereo release; a stereo mix of that track only surfaced later when reissue producer Phil Schaap located the previously unused master tape.

This is a vocal-forward orchestral recording, not a small-group jazz date, so string-section clarity and midrange warmth on Holiday's voice matter more than transient snap. The differences between pressings here are less about "which one is accurate" and more about surface noise, centering, and how much of the string section's upper harmonics survive to the outer grooves.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Columbia CL 1157 six-eye mono](https://www.discogs.com/release/1375064) – original 1958 first pressing, "T.M." rim print confirmed.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** [Analogue Productions APJ 144-45](https://www.discogs.com/release/25739833) – Bernie Grundman mastering from the original analogue tape, QRP pressing, 45rpm.

**Best-value modern:** [CBS 32259 UK reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/6605926) – a later Columbia/CBS UK repress, clean and detailed for the price.

---

## A note on the Analogue Productions pressing date

Labels and back-cover text on the Analogue Productions APJ 144-45 carry a 2022 copyright date, but the Discogs release entry for this exact pressing lists it as a 2023 release. Both details likely coexist – mastering/copyright registered in 2022, retail street date in 2023 – but I'm noting the discrepancy rather than asserting a single confident release year.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$ (Affordable):** The [**CBS 32259 UK reissue**](https://www.discogs.com/release/6605926) – the cheapest legitimate way into the album on vinyl; owner reviews describe it as clean and detailed for the price.

**$$$–$$$$ (Mid-range):** A clean [**Columbia CL 1157 six-eye mono**](https://www.discogs.com/release/1375064) original – condition and price vary a lot copy to copy given the album's age.

**$$$$ (Premium):** The [**Analogue Productions APJ 144-45**](https://www.discogs.com/release/25739833) 45rpm reissue – the best-sounding modern version, mastered directly from the original tape.
