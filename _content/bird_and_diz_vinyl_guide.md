# Bird and Diz on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Bird and Diz* documents two milestones in the same short program: the only time pianist Thelonious Monk ever recorded with Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie together, and the last time Parker and Gillespie appeared together in a studio at all. The bulk of the album was recorded June 6, 1950 in New York City, with Parker on alto sax, Gillespie on trumpet, Monk on piano, Curley Russell on bass, and Buddy Rich on drums, produced by Norman Granz. Five of the eight tracks are Parker originals, including "Bloomdido," "Relaxin' With Lee," and "An Oscar for Treadwell"; the sole standard is a ballad reading of "My Melancholy Baby." Two additional tracks, "Passport" and "Visa," were recorded earlier and separately – May 5 and February 3, 1949 respectively (Wikipedia's own summary prose and its detailed session table give slightly different months for this pair, and the table's day-level dates are treated as the more reliable source here) – by Parker alone, without Gillespie, and with different personnel (reportedly including Max Roach on drums on at least one session). Clef Records, Norman Granz's Verve subsidiary, originally issued the album in July or August 1952 as a 10" LP compiled from what had originally been individual 78rpm singles, catalog MG C-512.

Buddy Rich's presence on the date has been debated in drum forums, liner notes, and jazz journalism for seventy-five years, and it's a genuinely well-documented dispute rather than idle speculation. Rich came up firmly in the swing era, and several people close to the session believed he was a poor fit for bebop's rhythmic language – bassist Curley Russell reportedly called Rich's inclusion "one of Norman Granz's brainstorms," and drummer Roy Haynes, who had backed both Parker and Gillespie regularly through the mid-1940s, told biographer Carl Woideck that he'd been Parker's own stated preference for the date, only for Granz to substitute Rich anyway each time. Whatever the merits of that argument, the session captures something genuinely rare on tape: eleven separate takes of "Leap Frog" survive, seven of them false starts, preserving audible studio chatter between Parker and Gillespie as they worked the tune out in real time – a documented glimpse of two of bebop's foundational figures actively building a performance together, not just a finished, polished result.

DownBeat's original 1952 review awarded the album four stars and specifically praised Granz's production choices – notable given Granz was, at the time, better known for producing large-ensemble jazz-at-the-philharmonic style sessions rather than stripped-down quintet dates like this one. The tight, standard bebop instrumentation here – sax, trumpet, piano, bass, drums – is almost a deliberate change of pace from his usual approach.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Clef MG C-512](https://www.discogs.com/release/8684498) – 1952 original 10" pressing, the first US edition.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** the Verve Vital Vinyl series reissue – original label design and tape source, pressed in the Czech Republic; no specific mastering engineer was confirmed in sourcing, see note below.

**Best-value modern:** a later Verve 12" reissue under the "Genius of Charlie Parker" series banner (catalog MGV-8006 / MG V-8006).

## A more manageable catalog history than some other Parker-era titles

Unlike some other Charlie Parker reissues from this era, this title's catalog lineage is reasonably traceable rather than genuinely contradictory across sources. The 1952 10" Clef original (MG C-512) was expanded into a 12" LP reissue in 1956, retitled on the spine as "The Genius of Charlie Parker #4 – Bird and Diz" while the front cover and labels retained the original "Bird and Diz" title, issued as Verve MGV-8006 / MG V-8006 on yellow labels. That same catalog number was later repressed after 1960 on black Verve labels with deep groove and different rim text, sharing the same catalog number but a different label design and pressing generation.

The modern Verve Vital Vinyl series reissue markets itself with the tagline "original label, original tapes, original sound & design," pressed in the Czech Republic – but unlike many of the audiophile reissues covered elsewhere on this site, no specific mastering engineer is named in any retailer description found during this research pass. That's not necessarily a red flag on its own (plenty of legitimate reissues don't headline a celebrity mastering credit), but it does mean this guide can't make the same kind of specific, matrix-verified mastering claim for this pressing that it can for titles with named audiophile engineers – treat "original tapes" as the label's own marketing description rather than an independently confirmed technical fact.

## Sound quality by pressing

### Clef MG C-512 (1952, 10" original)
The first US pressing, on the original 10" format compiling what had been individual 78rpm singles. Given the format and era, surface noise and overall fidelity are inherently limited by 1950-vintage recording technology regardless of pressing condition – this is a historically essential document more than an audiophile showcase in its original form.

### Verve MGV-8006 (1956 12" reissue, "Genius of Charlie Parker #4")
Expanded onto a standard 12" LP, on yellow Verve labels for the earliest copies of this reissue and later black deep-groove labels for a subsequent pressing generation sharing the same catalog number. A real, findable way to own the complete program without hunting for the scarcer original 10".

### Verve Vital Vinyl series
Current in-print reissue, pressed in the Czech Republic, presented with the original label design and cover art. Owner reviews describe good, quiet, flat vinyl and praise the packaging, including the half-moon flip-back laminated jacket reproducing the original artwork – a legitimate, accessible way to hear the album on vinyl today, even without an independently confirmed mastering credit to point to.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$:** The Verve Vital Vinyl series reissue – accessible, well-packaged, and currently in print.

**$$$:** A Verve MGV-8006 12" reissue ("Genius of Charlie Parker #4"), either yellow-label or later black deep-groove pressing.

**$$$$:** A clean [Clef MG C-512](https://www.discogs.com/release/8684498) 10" original – the historically definitive first pressing, condition permitting given its age and format.
