# Bird and Diz on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Bird and Diz* is the only recording Thelonious Monk made with Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie together. It is also the last time Parker and Gillespie recorded together in a studio. Most of the album was recorded on June 6, 1950, in New York, produced by Norman Granz. Clef Records issued it in July or August 1952 as a 10" LP, MG C-512, compiled from 78rpm singles.

The recordings date from 1949 and 1950, so fidelity is limited on every pressing. The choice between editions turns mostly on track content, format and condition, not on mastering. The 1957 Verve 12" reissue drops two of the original tracks and adds alternate takes. The original 10" is the only way to hear the album as first issued.

- **Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Clef MG C-512](https://www.discogs.com/release/8684498). The 1952 10" original and first US edition.
- **Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** The Verve Vital Vinyl reissue. It reproduces the original label and cover and is pressed in the Czech Republic. No mastering engineer is credited.
- **Best-value modern:** [Verve MG V-8006](https://www.discogs.com/release/2189695), the 12" "Genius of Charlie Parker #4" reissue. It omits "Passport" and "Mohawk."

## Recording history and tape provenance

The June 1950 session had Parker on alto saxophone, Gillespie on trumpet, Monk on piano, Curley Russell on bass and Buddy Rich on drums. The Parker originals include "Bloomdido," "Relaxin' with Lee" and "An Oscar for Treadwell." The one standard is "My Melancholy Baby."

"Passport" and "Visa" were recorded separately in 1949 by Parker without Gillespie. According to [uDiscover Music](https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-bird-and-diz/), "Passport" features Kenny Dorham, Al Haig, Tommy Potter and Max Roach. "Visa" adds Tommy Turk on trombone and Carlos Vidal on bongos. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_and_Diz) dates the two sessions to March and May 1949.

Buddy Rich's place on the date has long been disputed, as [JazzTimes](https://www.jazztimes.com/features/special-feature/buddy-rich-and-the-bird-and-diz-album-the-controversy-continues/) covers. Rich came from swing, and some around the session thought him a poor fit for bebop. Curley Russell reportedly called his inclusion one of Granz's brainstorms.

A 1952 DownBeat article quoted Roy Haynes saying Granz had refused to let him record with Parker. Haynes said Parker would propose his own musicians and Granz would overrule him. [GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/bird-and-diz-album-jazz-bebop-70-anniversary-buddy-rich-thelonious-monk-historical-feature/) reports the article, citing Parker biographer Carl Woideck.

Eleven takes of "Leap Frog" survive, seven of them false starts. They preserve studio talk between Parker and Gillespie as they worked the tune out.

## Original Clef pressing (1952)

[Clef MG C-512](https://www.discogs.com/release/8684498) is the 10" first US pressing, compiled from what had been 78rpm singles. Surface noise and fidelity reflect the 1949–1950 recordings on any copy. It is an essential historical document more than an audiophile record.

## Verve reissues

[Verve MG V-8006](https://www.discogs.com/release/2189695) is the 12" reissue in the "Genius of Charlie Parker" series, numbered #4. The spine reads "The Genius of Charlie Parker #4 – Bird and Diz," while the front cover and labels keep the original title. Early copies are on yellow Verve labels. After 1960 the same number was repressed on black Verve labels with a deep groove and different rim text.

According to [uDiscover Music](https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/rediscover-bird-and-diz/), Verve issued this reissue in 1957, after Parker's death. It left out "Passport" and "Mohawk" and added alternate takes of other tracks. It is not the complete original program.

The Verve Vital Vinyl reissue is currently in print, pressed in the Czech Republic with the original label and cover design. Its marketing describes original label, original tapes, original sound and design. No mastering engineer is named in retailer descriptions, so the tape source rests on the label's own description. Owner reviews describe quiet, flat vinyl and praise the laminated flip-back jacket.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$:** The Verve Vital Vinyl reissue. Accessible, well packaged and in print.

**$$$:** A [Verve MG V-8006](https://www.discogs.com/release/2189695) 12" reissue on yellow or later black deep-groove labels. It lacks "Passport" and "Mohawk."

**$$$$:** A clean [Clef MG C-512](https://www.discogs.com/release/8684498) 10" original. The first pressing and the only one with the original program, condition permitting.
