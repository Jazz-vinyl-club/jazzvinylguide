# Ella Fitzgerald Sings the Cole Porter Songbook on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

This album was recorded at Capitol Studios in Hollywood on February 7, 8 and 9, 1956, with a fourth session on March 27. Norman Granz produced, and Buddy Bregman arranged and conducted a full studio orchestra through 32 Cole Porter songs. Verve released it on May 15, 1956 as MG V-4001-2. It was the first album on Granz's new label and the first of eight Songbook volumes.

The recording is mono, and no contemporaneous stereo mix exists. Multitrack stereo was not standard for Verve sessions this early. A listing describing this title as stereo needs an explanation of where that mix came from. Two modern reissues matter, a 2016 three-LP box and a 2026 two-LP set, and they differ in mastering, plant and layout.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Verve MG V-4001-2](https://www.discogs.com/release/6304011), the 1956 mono original.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** The [Analog Spark AS00007 3LP box](https://www.discogs.com/release/9437368-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book) (2016), cut by Ryan K. Smith at Sterling Sound from the mono masters.

**Best-value modern:** The [Verve Acoustic Sounds Series edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/37743816-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book) (2026), mastered by Matthew Lutthans.

## Recording history and tape provenance

Granz had just founded Verve and managed Fitzgerald. He avoided another small-combo session in the mould of her Decca work. Her first Verve release was built around one songwriter's catalogue, with a full orchestra.

He explained the thinking plainly. He wanted Fitzgerald heard as more than a singer for jazz fans. First-rate songbook material with enough jazz inflection in the backing would do it.

The 32 songs were recorded live with the orchestra across the four sessions. The band included Harry "Sweets" Edison and Maynard Ferguson on trumpet, Bud Shank on alto saxophone and Ted Nash on tenor and flute.

The Songbook series ran to 1964, with volumes for Rodgers and Hart, Duke Ellington, Irving Berlin, the Gershwins, Harold Arlen, Jerome Kern, and Johnny Mercer.

## Original Verve pressings (1956)

[Verve MG V-4001-2](https://www.discogs.com/release/6304011) is the 1956 mono first pressing, a two-LP set in a gatefold. Verve spread manufacturing across several plants, and Discogs documents them separately.

US plant variants include [Indianapolis](https://www.discogs.com/release/6304011), [Rockaway](https://www.discogs.com/release/1377840-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book) and [Hollywood](https://www.discogs.com/release/12381644-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book) pressings. A separate entry documents an [orange label with black "T"](https://www.discogs.com/release/22061839-Ella-Fitzgerald-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Songbook) copy.

The album was also pressed in 1956 in the [Netherlands](https://www.discogs.com/release/7910887-Ella-Fitzgerald-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Songbook), [Australia](https://www.discogs.com/release/9282087-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book) and [South Africa](https://www.discogs.com/release/8992430-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book). None of these has a published sound comparison with a US copy.

Verve reissued it around 1960 under the shortened number V-4001-2, including an [MGM Custom pressing](https://www.discogs.com/release/7957808-Ella-Fitzgerald-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book). The record sold well and stayed in print, so worn copies are common and clean ones are not.

## Analog Spark AS00007 (2016)

Analog Spark issued a 60th-anniversary box for the album's 60th year. [AnalogPlanet](https://www.analogplanet.com/node/120523) reports that Ryan K. Smith cut lacquers at Sterling Sound from the original mono master tapes. The 32 songs are spread across three RTI-pressed records rather than the original two.

Spreading the same music over six sides widens groove spacing, which generally helps a record carrying this much orchestral material. The original gatefold becomes a laminated lift-top box, with the gatefold annotation printed inside.

The [AnalogPlanet review](https://www.analogplanet.com/node/120523) prefers the reissue to the original in strong terms. Comments there note a recurring complaint that Analog Spark reissues run bright, raised about other titles in the line rather than this one.

The box has its own [Discogs entry](https://www.discogs.com/release/9437368-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book), whose runouts read "B0024939-01A RKS STERLING" and so on across the six sides. Those etchings confirm the Ryan K. Smith credit from the record itself.

One owner who has four LP issues of the album plus the Verve CD rates this box the best sounding of them. That owner also reports US pressing quality below European standards, with occasional pops.

## Verve Acoustic Sounds Series AVER 40304 (2026)

[Acoustic Sounds](https://store.acousticsounds.com/d/197406/) lists this two-LP mono reissue as mastered by Matthew Lutthans at The Mastering Lab from the original analog master tape. It is pressed on 180g at Quality Record Pressings, in Stoughton tip-on gatefold jackets. Its Discogs entry is [here](https://www.discogs.com/release/37743816-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book), under catalogue V-4001-2.

The mastering credit was worth checking. Several retailers used copy crediting Ryan K. Smith, which reads as boilerplate for the wider Acoustic Sounds Series rather than a credit for this title. Owner reviews on the Discogs page credit Lutthans by name, which matches the label's own listing.

Those owner reviews are mostly strong, describing flat, quiet records with Ella forward and warm. Two temper that. One finds the bass muddled and the orchestra short of stage depth against other Acoustic Sounds titles, and another notes the package includes no tracklist.

## Later reissues

Verve reissued the album in 1976 as a two-LP set numbered [VE-2-2511](https://www.discogs.com/release/10105752-Ella-Fitzgerald-The-Cole-Porter-Songbook), retitled *The Cole Porter Songbook*. Several pressings of that number exist, including one [listed as stereo](https://www.discogs.com/release/12200567-Ella-Fitzgerald-The-Cole-Porter-Songbook).

The recording is mono, so any stereo-labelled reissue is a reprocessing or a mislabel. Discogs lists stereo copies from [Canada in 1984](https://www.discogs.com/release/5502420-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Songbook) and a [180g limited edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/8059756-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book) numbered 772112. Neither states where the stereo came from.

A [1983 Japanese pressing](https://www.discogs.com/release/3602378-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Songbook), catalogue 35MJ 3266/7, is mono and in a gatefold. No published comparison with a US copy has been found.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$$$:** A clean [Verve MG V-4001-2](https://www.discogs.com/release/6304011) original, priced on condition and plant.

**$$$$$:** The [Analog Spark AS00007 3LP box](https://www.discogs.com/release/9437368-Ella-Fitzgerald-Sings-The-Cole-Porter-Song-Book), for the wider groove spacing.
