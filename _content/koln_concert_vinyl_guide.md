# The Köln Concert on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*The Köln Concert* was recorded live on January 24, 1975 at the Cologne Opera House, produced by Manfred Eicher and engineered by Martin Wieland. Keith Jarrett improvised the whole performance on solo piano. ECM released it on November 30, 1975 as ECM 1064/65 ST, a two-LP set. It became the best-selling solo piano album in history, and in 2025 it entered the Library of Congress National Recording Registry.

The 2010 Pallas pressing is the one to buy. The newest official edition, the 2025 50th Anniversary set, has drawn unusually critical reviews. ECM also declines to say whether any of its reissues are cut from analog or digital sources. No modern pressing of this album is confirmed all-analog. That makes the 2010 Pallas cut a listening recommendation rather than a documented one.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** An [original German pressing](https://www.discogs.com/release/8735978-Keith-Jarrett-The-K%C3%B6ln-Concert) with the "ECM Records" logo and "Urheber-" rim text, in a laminated gatefold.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** The [2010 German 180g reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/3979641-Keith-Jarrett-The-K%C3%B6ln-Concert), pressed at Pallas.

**Best-value modern:** The same 2010 Pallas pressing, which is widely available and consistently praised.

## Recording history and tape provenance

Jarrett was 29, already established through Charles Lloyd's quartet, Miles Davis's electric bands and ECM solo records including *Facing You* and *Bremen/Lausanne*.

Vera Brandes, then 18 and already Germany's youngest professional concert promoter, had organised the first jazz concert ever staged at the Köln Opera House. It was scheduled for 11:30pm, after an evening performance of Alban Berg's *Lulu*.

She had ordered a Bösendorfer 290 Imperial concert grand. Opera house staff wheeled out a smaller rehearsal instrument instead. It was battered and out of tune, with sticking keys in the upper register and pedals that did not work.

Jarrett had driven overnight from Zurich and was in severe back pain. He nearly refused to play, relenting only after Brandes and piano tuners made the instrument minimally playable.

The piano's faults shaped the performance. Jarrett avoided the tinny upper registers and worked in the lower-mid range with propulsive left-hand ostinato figures.

[Tim Harford's *Cautionary Tales*](https://timharford.com/2019/09/cautionary-tales-ep-1-too-big-to-fail/) describes the result as almost trance-like, with rumbling repetitive bass riffs compensating for the piano's lack of resonance.

Part I opens with a simple motif in G major that expands over 26 minutes. Part II, spread across three sides, is more urgent and rhythmically driven. Brandes recalled that everybody in the room knew this was no longer the Cologne opera but a cathedral.

Eicher later reflected that Jarrett probably played it the way he did because it was not a good piano. Being unable to fall in love with the sound made him find another way to get the most out of it.

Jarrett suffered two severe strokes in 2018 that left him partially paralysed and unable to play with his left hand. He has not performed publicly since.

The 2025 film [*Köln 75*](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt14852728/), directed by Ido Fluk with John Magaro as Jarrett and Mala Emde as Brandes, dramatises the evening. It was made without Jarrett's cooperation and features none of the original concert music.

## The tape and the format

Wieland recorded the concert with two Neumann U 67 microphones, giving good fidelity for a live recording of the period. The piano was a Bösendorfer, worn in the upper registers and weakly voiced in the bass.

The tape is in good condition and has been the source for all authorised reissues.

The two-LP gatefold, with Part I on the first disc and Parts IIa, IIb and IIc on the second, is the original and correct presentation.

## What ECM will not say about its sources

ECM does not publicly specify the mastering chain for its reissues of this title, including the well-regarded 2010 Pallas pressing.

[Tracking Angle](https://trackingangle.com/features/ecm-releases-50th-anniversary-edition-of-keith-jarretts-groundbreaking-the-koln-concert) recounts being told by an ECM representative that the label does not consider the analog-against-digital distinction worth tracking.

So no modern pressing of this album can be described as confirmed all-analog. Collector regard for the 2010 pressing rests on how it sounds rather than on a documented chain.

## Original German pressings (1975)

The earliest copies carry the full "ECM Records" logo on orange labels and "Urheber-" text in the rim, an early German pressing convention. They come in a laminated gatefold with a short runout in the ECM 1064/65 A to D matrix area.

[Later German copies](https://www.discogs.com/release/14010199-Keith-Jarrett-The-K%C3%B6ln-Concert) from 1976 and 1977 replace "ECM Records" with "ECM" alone and update the rim text to "Alle Urheber- Und Leistungsschutzrechte." The gatefold is matte rather than laminated.

Both come from the same plant and the same metalwork, so they are sonic equivalents. The "Urheber-" copies command a small premium for being first.

Later German pressings from the Polydor distribution era can be identified by different runout matrix stamps.

## The 2010 Pallas reissue

The [2010 reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/3979641-Keith-Jarrett-The-K%C3%B6ln-Concert) is a 180g two-LP set pressed at Pallas in Germany, long regarded as among the finest plants in Europe.

Collector regard for it is high and consistent. ECM has not confirmed the mastering chain, so that regard rests on listening rather than documentation.

It is widely available and the best combination of accessibility and fidelity for this title.

## The 2025 50th Anniversary edition

The [50th Anniversary edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/35912773-Keith-Jarrett-The-K%C3%B6ln-Concert) is a 180g two-LP set in a tip-on gatefold. It adds an eight-page booklet and an art print carrying a printed Jarrett signature.

Reception has been more critical than most ECM reissues. [Tracking Angle](https://trackingangle.com/music/keith-jarretts-legendary-koln-concert-gets-an-ecm-reissue) identifies Daniel Krieger at SST as the mastering engineer and finds the result sounds like a dynamically compressed file.

[That review](https://trackingangle.com/music/keith-jarretts-legendary-koln-concert-gets-an-ecm-reissue) describes the notes as clangy and lacking harmonic structure. It finds the reverb fails to define the space the way an original German pressing does. Several commenters independently prefer their original German copies.

This is a commemorative package rather than a sonic upgrade.

## US and Japanese pressings

The [US Polydor pressing](https://www.discogs.com/release/1259362-Keith-Jarrett-The-K%C3%B6ln-Concert), catalogue 2-25002, was mastered at Sterling Sound and pressed at various US plants from ECM-supplied masters. Owners describe it as slightly drier than the German pressings.

The [Japanese Polydor and Trio pressing](https://www.discogs.com/release/490628-Keith-Jarrett-The-K%C3%B6ln-Concert), catalogue PA-6053 and PA-6054, was distributed by Trio-Kenwood. It has Japanese liner notes and an Eicher interview printed on the obi.

Collector opinion splits against the German original. One owner calls it too perfect, finding the German pressing livelier with more dynamics and audible room ambience. It is quiet-vinyl and well regarded regardless.

## Pressings to avoid

UK and other non-German European pressings from the 1970s and 1980s were made at local plants from copy tapes rather than the original German metal. Quality varies, and the 2010 Pallas pressing beats most of them at the same price or less.

Several generic vinyl reissues from the 2000s onwards were pressed from digital sources. The pressing plant and source are worth verifying before buying a budget copy.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$:** The [2010 ECM 180g Pallas pressing](https://www.discogs.com/release/3979641-Keith-Jarrett-The-K%C3%B6ln-Concert), the recommended listening copy.

**$$:** The [50th Anniversary edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/35912773-Keith-Jarrett-The-K%C3%B6ln-Concert), for the packaging rather than the sound.

**$$$:** A clean original ["ECM Records" first pressing](https://www.discogs.com/release/8735978-Keith-Jarrett-The-K%C3%B6ln-Concert), with the "Urheber-" rim text and short runout confirmed.
