# Black Codes (From the Underground) on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Black Codes (From the Underground)* was recorded at RCA Studio A in New York in January 1985, produced by Steve Epstein and George Butler. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Codes_(From_the_Underground)) gives the dates as January 7 to 11 and 14, while Discogs entries list January 11 and 14. It was remixed and mastered at CBS Recording Studios. Columbia released it on June 9, 1985 as FC 40009.

The album was recorded digitally on a Sony PCM-3324 multitrack, so no analog master tape exists. Any reissue is a digital source cut to lacquer, whatever the marketing says, so mastering and pressing quality are the only real variables between editions. Two modern reissues exist alongside the 1985 original, and the original itself came from several plants. Original copies remain common and cheap, which shapes the buying decision more than usual.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** An original [Columbia FC 40009](https://www.discogs.com/release/395747-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground) (1985). Carrollton and Pitman variants are both well regarded.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** The [2014 180g reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/5793563-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground), which an owner review credits to Bernie Grundman and a Pallas pressing.

**Best-value modern:** A clean original FC 40009. They are common and inexpensive.

## Recording history and tape provenance

The quintet is Marsalis on trumpet and Branford Marsalis on tenor and soprano saxophone. Kenny Kirkland plays piano, Jeff "Tain" Watts drums and Charnett Moffett bass. Kirkland wrote "Chambers of Tain." Ron Carter plays bass on "Aural Oasis," a role he had also taken on the previous year's *Hot House Flowers*.

This was Marsalis's sixth album and the first built almost entirely on his own compositions.

The session used a Sony PCM-3324 digital multitrack and an MCI recording console. A Neve automated console handled the remix, per the credits on its [Discogs entries](https://www.discogs.com/release/27919077-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground). Microphones included Neumann U-67 and KM-84 and AKG 414 and 451 models.

Because the recording chain was digital from the start, no all-analog reissue of this title is possible. A pressing described as cut from the original tapes would still be a digital source. The 2023 Vinyl Me, Please edition states as much on its hype sticker, which reads DAA.

## Original Columbia pressings (1985)

[Columbia FC 40009](https://www.discogs.com/release/395747-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground) was pressed at several plants. The Carrollton pressing has runouts etched AL 40009 and BL 40009 with VLADO in the deadwax. Owner reviews describe it as sounding excellent.

A [Pitman pressing](https://www.discogs.com/release/14587364-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground) carries P (mp) and P (T) markings in the runouts, in several documented variants. Owner reviews are also positive.

A [Dutch pressing](https://www.discogs.com/release/1541225-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground) exists for European buyers, with a printed inner sleeve and STEMRA rights markings. Originals in clean condition are common and cheap.

## Modern reissues

The [2014 180g reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/5793563-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground) is described in a detailed owner review as mastered by Bernie Grundman and pressed at Pallas in Germany. The same review calls the vinyl very quiet, with a low noise floor and wide dynamic range. Those credits come from an owner, not from the release's own credits.

[Vinyl Me, Please Classics](https://www.discogs.com/release/27919077-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground) issued a 180g gatefold edition in August 2023, catalogue 19658795151, with a listening booklet and an obi strip. Owner reviews praise the sound and the pressing quality.

The same reviews criticise the artwork reproduction, calling the gatefold photo low-resolution. One owner notes that clean originals are neither scarce nor expensive, which is worth weighing against the reissue price.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$:** A clean original [Columbia FC 40009](https://www.discogs.com/release/395747-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground), in any plant variant.

**$$$:** The [2014 180g reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/5793563-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground), for the quietest surfaces reported by owners.

**$$$:** The [2023 Vinyl Me, Please Classics](https://www.discogs.com/release/27919077-Wynton-Marsalis-Black-Codes-From-The-Underground) edition, for the gatefold package, with weaker artwork reproduction.
