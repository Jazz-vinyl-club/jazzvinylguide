# Black Codes (From the Underground) on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Black Codes (From the Underground)* was recorded January 7–11 and 14, 1985 at RCA Studio in New York City, produced by Steve Epstein and George Butler, remixed and mastered at CBS Recording Studios. It was Wynton Marsalis's sixth album overall but the first to consist almost entirely of his own compositions – previous records had mixed in standards alongside one or two originals. The quintet is Marsalis on trumpet, his brother Branford on tenor and soprano sax, Kenny Kirkland on piano (who also wrote "Chambers of Tain"), Jeff "Tain" Watts on drums, and Charnett Moffett – a teenager at the time – on bass for most tracks, with Ron Carter specifically covering bass on "Aural Oasis," continuing a role he'd also played on the previous year's *Hot House Flowers*. Released June 9, 1985 as Columbia FC 40009, the album won two Grammy Awards in 1986 (Best Instrumental Jazz Performance, Individual or Group, and Best Jazz Instrumental Performance, Soloist) and was inducted into the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry in 2023, which described it as one of Marsalis's "most beloved & artistically successful recordings, hearkening back to midcentury acoustic jazz but with a distinctly 1980s flair."

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** an original Columbia FC 40009 (1985) – see the note below on the recording's fully digital origin before assuming any reissue could offer an "all-analog" alternative.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** not confirmed in this pass – see note below.

**Best-value modern:** any clean original Columbia FC 40009 pressing, given the lack of a confirmed dedicated audiophile reissue.

## A real technical fact worth knowing: there is no analog master tape

Unlike every vintage recording covered elsewhere on this site, this album was recorded fully digitally from the start, using a Sony PCM-3324 digital multitrack recorder, then remixed and mastered digitally at CBS's New York studios. That means there is no analog master tape for any future reissue to remaster from, however it's marketed – a meaningful difference from vinyl reissues of 1950s-70s recordings, where "cut from the original analog tapes" is a genuine, verifiable claim. Any future audiophile vinyl reissue of this title would, at best, be a digital-to-analog cut (sometimes called ADA – analog disc from digital tape), not a true all-analog (AAA) release, regardless of how it's marketed. This isn't a knock on the recording's quality – contemporary reviews and its Grammy wins speak for themselves – just a fact worth knowing if you're specifically chasing "all-analog" mastering the way you might for the site's earlier-era guides.

An Acoustic Sounds product listing for a 180-gram vinyl edition of this title exists but was not confirmed as an actually-shipped release with a specific mastering credit at the time of this research – treat it as an unconfirmed lead rather than a real, currently available reissue until that's verified.

## Sound quality by pressing

### Columbia FC 40009 (1985 original)
Documented pressing-plant variants include Carrollton and Pitman manufacturing, both reported by owners as sounding excellent. Given the fully digital recording chain, differences between individual original pressings are more likely to come down to standard vinyl-pressing quality control than any "which tape generation" question relevant to older analog titles.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$:** A clean original Columbia FC 40009 pressing, any documented pressing-plant variant (Carrollton, Pitman) – both reported as sounding excellent, and there's no confirmed audiophile alternative to weigh against it.
