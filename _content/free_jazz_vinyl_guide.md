# Free Jazz on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Free Jazz: A Collective Improvisation* was recorded on December 21, 1960 at A&R Studios in New York, produced by Nesuhi Ertegun. Two complete jazz quartets play at once in the same room, improvising for over 36 minutes with no predetermined structure, no splicing and no overdubs. Atlantic released it in September 1961 as 1364 in mono and SD-1364 in stereo.

The two quartets are hard-panned to opposite channels, which makes the stereo and mono editions different listening propositions rather than variants. Channel separation is also the reason a 45rpm cut suits this record. One currently in-print reissue has a documented pressing defect, reported by many owners and absent from others.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Atlantic 1364 mono](https://www.discogs.com/release/1942501) or [SD-1364 stereo](https://www.discogs.com/release/370754), the 1961 originals.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** The ORG Music 45rpm 2LP, cut by Bernie Grundman from the original analog tapes and pressed at Pallas.

**Best-value modern:** The [Speakers Corner 180g reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/20407990), with the non-fill reports in mind.

## Recording history and tape provenance

Coleman's working quartet occupies the left channel. It is Coleman on alto saxophone, Don Cherry on pocket trumpet, Scott LaFaro on bass and Billy Higgins on drums. The right channel has the second quartet. That is Eric Dolphy on bass clarinet, Freddie Hubbard on trumpet, Charlie Haden on bass and Ed Blackwell on drums.

The title named a genre, though the term had existed earlier with an unrelated meaning, describing jazz performed without an admission charge.

The cover reproduces Jackson Pollock's painting "White Light," chosen as a visual analogy for the music. The concept later inspired the artwork for *Promises*, the 2021 Floating Points and Pharoah Sanders album.

## Mono against stereo

The stereo SD-1364 delivers the channel separation Coleman designed the session around. The two quartets stay distinct, which is the point of the recording.

The mono 1364 presents the same performance without that separation. Some listeners prefer it for a more unified sound, and one Discogs commenter reports that mono originals play reliably even in rough condition.

These are not interchangeable. A buyer should decide which version of the album they want before comparing pressings.

## Original Atlantic pressings (1961)

[Atlantic 1364](https://www.discogs.com/release/1942501) in mono has an orange and purple label with a white fan logo on black, in a die-cut gatefold jacket. One documented copy carries the matrices "11807-A AT. W J" and "11808-A AT. W J."

[Atlantic SD-1364](https://www.discogs.com/release/370754) in stereo has a blue and green label with the same white fan logo.

Later Atlantic pressings from the 1970s to 1990s share the SD-1364 number with different label designs. They are a cheap way in for a buyer not chasing an original.

## The Speakers Corner non-fill problem

[Speakers Corner's 2021 reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/20407990), catalogue AATL 1364, is all-analog from the original tapes, pressed at Pallas in Germany, reproducing the original artwork and die-cut gatefold.

Multiple owner reviews of this pressing report non-fill. That is a manufacturing defect where molten vinyl does not completely fill the stamper. It leaves audible dropouts or missing groove sections rather than ordinary surface noise.

Reports range from no issues at all to far too much non-fill on the same catalogue number. That pattern points to inconsistent quality control across the run rather than a defect in every copy.

An owner review of the ORG Music 45rpm on the [Discogs master](https://www.discogs.com/master/28578) confirms the problem from the other side. That buyer chose the ORG edition specifically because non-fill on their Speakers Corner copy drove them to find an alternative.

A separate Discogs comment on the [master page](https://www.discogs.com/master/28578) is harsher about the title's reissue history in general, saying only a small number of the many reissues reach acceptable quality.

## ORG Music 45rpm

The ORG Music edition is a 2LP cut at 45rpm by Bernie Grundman from the original analog tapes, pressed at Pallas.

ORG's own description argues the format suits this record specifically. Because the album is two hard-panned channels rather than a blended mix, wider groove spacing helps keep the quartets distinct instead of bleeding into each other. That is the label's reasoning, and it is consistent with how the session was recorded.

## Japanese pressing

A 1972 Japanese pressing circulates among collectors. One reviewer on the [Discogs master](https://www.discogs.com/master/28578) describes it as sounding pretty good. No catalogue number for it could be confirmed, so it carries no tier.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$:** A used 1970s to 1990s Atlantic pressing under the SD-1364 number.

**$$$:** The [Speakers Corner 180g reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/20407990), inspected on arrival or bought with a clear return policy.

**$$$$:** A clean original [Atlantic 1364 mono](https://www.discogs.com/release/1942501) or [SD-1364 stereo](https://www.discogs.com/release/370754), priced on condition.

**$$$$$:** The ORG Music 45rpm 2LP, for the channel separation the record was built on.
