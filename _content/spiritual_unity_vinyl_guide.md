# Spiritual Unity on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Spiritual Unity* was recorded on July 10, 1964 at the Variety Arts Recording Studio in New York, produced by Bernard Stollman. It was the first jazz record ESP-Disk released. The trio is Albert Ayler on tenor saxophone, Gary Peacock on bass and Sunny Murray on drums. ESP-Disk issued it in January 1965 as catalogue 1002.

The five tracks are "Ghosts (First Variation)," "The Wizard," "Spirits," "Units" and "Ghosts (Second Variation)." One complication matters more than any pressing variant. A different performance was substituted for "Spirits" on an early vinyl edition, so some copies do not contain the 1964 take. Which take a copy holds matters more than its label variant or pressing year.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [ESP 1002 mono](https://www.discogs.com/release/4339219), the first pressing, with a Plastylite "ear" in the dead wax.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** The current ESP-Disk repress, 180g with the original artwork restored, listed by [Forced Exposure](https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/ayler-trio-albert-spiritual-unity-lp/ESPDISK.1002LP.html) as a 2024 repress.

**Best-value modern:** The same ESP-Disk repress. Several vintage European pressings are poorly made.

## Recording history and tape provenance

Stollman has described first hearing Ayler at the Baby Grand Café on 125th Street in Harlem, during a Sunday afternoon set by Elmo Hope's trio. Ayler climbed onto the stage uninvited and played. Hope's group stopped to listen.

Stollman approached him afterwards, said he was starting a label and asked him to be its first artist. Ayler agreed but had a prior commitment until the following spring. He called that June.

The session took place in a small Latin-music studio off Broadway. Peacock had recently been playing with Paul Bley and had recorded with Bill Evans. Murray had played with Ayler in Cecil Taylor's group.

Peacock and Murray do not accompany Ayler in the conventional sense. Murray's playing is cymbal-heavy and abandons a steady pulse, and Peacock's bass lines are freely melodic rather than time-keeping.

## The substituted "Spirits" track

ESP-Disk's own description of the 50th Anniversary Expanded Edition is on [Bandcamp](https://albertayler.bandcamp.com/album/spiritual-unity). It states that a different performance was briefly and accidentally substituted for "Spirits" on an early vinyl edition.

That substituted track is the tune issued as "Vibrations" on the Arista/Freedom album of that name, and as "Ghosts" on Debut. It comes from a different Ayler session, so it is not an alternate take of the same performance.

ESP-Disk corrected the error on later editions. The 50th Anniversary reissue includes the substituted performance as a bonus track so both appear together. A detailed Discogs contributor comment on the [master page](https://www.discogs.com/master/39790-Albert-Ayler-Trio-Spiritual-Unity) sets out which pressings carry which take.

Anyone who wants the 1964 session as recorded should check which "Spirits" a copy contains before buying.

## Original ESP-Disk pressings (1965)

[ESP 1002](https://www.discogs.com/release/4339219) in mono is the first pressing. It has a deep groove on side A, a Plastylite "ear" in the dead wax and an "sf" lacquer credit for Bell Sound. The front cover carries no catalogue number or ESP address, which appear only on the label. Early copies included a booklet.

A [stereo pressing](https://www.discogs.com/release/7625206-Albert-Ayler-Trio-Spiritual-Unity) carries ESPS 1002 in the runouts, also with Plastylite ears. ESP-Disk's production standards in this period were low-budget, so condition varies more than usual between copies.

## European pressings

Base Record in Italy issued the album with the packaging and labels disagreeing with each other. The [sleeve reads "monaural" and catalogue 1002, while the labels read "stereo" and ESPS 1002](https://www.discogs.com/release/1210930-Albert-Ayler-Trio-Spiritual-Unity). An owner calls it badly pressed and of dubious legality. A [second Base Record variant](https://www.discogs.com/release/24684191-Albert-Ayler-Trio-Spiritual-Unity) differs in label printing.

The Italian [Get Back reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/402263-Albert-Ayler-Trio-Spiritual-Unity) is a 180g pressing whose sleeve notes say the artwork follows the original ESP edition. Owner comments report skipping on clean-looking copies and an audible tone partway through "Ghosts" on side B.

## Current ESP-Disk repress

ESP-Disk has kept the album in print on 180g vinyl with the original artwork restored. [Forced Exposure](https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/ayler-trio-albert-spiritual-unity-lp/ESPDISK.1002LP.html) lists the current stock as a 2024 repress, and earlier dealer listings describe a 2018 repress pressed in the United States.

No mastering engineer is credited for these repressings, and no published comparison with an original has been found. They are the simplest way to own the album without navigating the vintage European editions.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$:** The current [ESP-Disk repress](https://www.forcedexposure.com/Catalog/ayler-trio-albert-spiritual-unity-lp/ESPDISK.1002LP.html), in print and straightforward.

**$$$$:** A clean [ESP 1002 mono](https://www.discogs.com/release/4339219) original, priced on condition, with the correct "Spirits" confirmed.
