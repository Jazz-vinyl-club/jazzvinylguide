# At the Pershing: But Not for Me on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*At the Pershing: But Not for Me* was recorded January 16, 1958 at the Pershing Lounge in Chicago's Pershing Hotel, where Ahmad Jamal's trio – with Israel Crosby on bass and Vernell Fournier on drums – had been holding a residency since 1956. Every set played that night was recorded, 43 tracks in total, from which Jamal selected eight for the album. Sources differ on the producer credit: Wikipedia lists Dave Usher, while MusicBrainz lists Phil Chess (of Chess Records, Argo's parent label) – both were plausibly involved given the label's structure at the time, but this guide isn't asserting one over the other with confidence.

Released later that year as Argo LP-628 (mono, with a stereo edition following as LPS-628), the album became a genuine commercial phenomenon rather than a niche jazz release: it spent 108 weeks in Billboard's Top Ten album chart and has reportedly sold over a million copies, according to Jamal himself. "Poinciana," the album's closing track, became Jamal's signature piece – a hypnotic, spacious interplay between Crosby's bass and Jamal's piano that jazz critics and musicians (including Miles Davis, who cited Jamal's rhythmic and spatial approach as a direct influence on his own thinking) have returned to for decades. Jazzwise ranked the album No. 21 on its list of 100 Jazz Albums That Shook the World. A companion release, *At the Pershing, Vol. 2* (Argo LP-667), followed in 1961, drawn from the same January 1958 engagement but a distinct release in its own right rather than a reissue of the same material.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Argo LP-628](https://www.discogs.com/release/371461) – 1958 original mono pressing, black labels, deep groove.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** the Analogue Productions 200-gram reissue – see the note below on a real discrepancy between its marketing claims and its actual dead-wax evidence before treating "mastered at Sterling Sound" as settled fact.

**Best-value modern:** a later Chess/Sugar Hill reissue (catalog CH-91081, 1984) – a straightforward, more affordable stereo repress.

## A marketing claim contradicted by the dead wax

Every retailer description of the current Analogue Productions reissue states plainly that it was "mastered at Sterling Sound from the original tapes." That claim doesn't survive contact with the record's own runout inscriptions. A detailed Discogs listing for the 2020 pressing of this reissue reports that, per the actual matrix inscriptions cut into the vinyl, the release uses pre-existing metalwork dating to a lacquer cut done by Trutone Mastering back in December 1983 – not a new cut made specifically for this release, and not by Sterling Sound at all. A separate owner, independently examining the same pressing, raised the identical doubt from a different angle: unable to find engineer initials matching any known Sterling Sound mastering engineer on the runout, despite the promotional claim. Two independent people, working from the physical evidence rather than the marketing copy, arrived at the same conclusion.

This doesn't necessarily mean the reissue sounds bad – multiple owner reviews describe it as flat, quiet, and true to a good-sounding 1958 live tape, "not a demonstration album" but a legitimate, well-produced pressing of a fairly modest source recording. The issue is specifically the mastering-credit claim, not the record's actual listenability. Treat "mastered at Sterling Sound from the original tapes" as unverified promotional language rather than a confirmed technical fact for this specific pressing, pending a clearer explanation from Analogue Productions of how 1983 Trutone metalwork and a 2020s Sterling Sound credit fit together.

## Sound quality by pressing

### Argo LP-628 (1958 original)
The true first pressing, on black labels with deep groove. Given the recording's status as a working-club live tape from 1958 rather than a pristine studio session, don't expect audiophile-grade fidelity from any pressing of this title, original or reissue – multiple listeners note the recording itself has inherent surface-level limitations (light background noise, a modest dynamic range) that no remastering can fully transcend, though a clean original still captures the trio's interplay vividly.

### Analogue Productions 200-gram reissue
Plated and pressed at Quality Record Pressings in a Stoughton old-style tip-on jacket – solid production values regardless of the mastering-credit question above. Owner reviews consistently describe it as quiet and well-produced, if unspectacular as a sonic showcase, which is consistent with the source recording's own limitations rather than any fault of this specific pressing.

### Chess/Sugar Hill reissue (1984)
A straightforward later stereo reissue on blue and silver Chess labels, catalog CH-91081. A reasonable, affordable way into the album without chasing a 1958 original or paying current audiophile-reissue prices.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$:** The Chess/Sugar Hill reissue (1984, catalog CH-91081) – straightforward and affordable.

**$$$:** The Analogue Productions 200-gram reissue – solid production values, though treat the specific "Sterling Sound" mastering claim with the skepticism outlined above.

**$$$$:** A clean [Argo LP-628](https://www.discogs.com/release/371461) 1958 original, condition-dependent – the historically definitive document of this performance.
