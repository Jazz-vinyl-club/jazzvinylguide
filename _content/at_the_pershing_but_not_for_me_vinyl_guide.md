# At the Pershing: But Not for Me on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*At the Pershing: But Not for Me* was recorded on January 16, 1958 at the Pershing Lounge in Chicago's Pershing Hotel. Ahmad Jamal's trio, with Israel Crosby on bass and Vernell Fournier on drums, had held a residency there since 1956. Argo released it later that year as LP-628 in mono. It spent 108 weeks in Billboard's top ten, and Jamal has said it sold over a million copies.

Two things complicate buying it. The original master tapes are reported lost in a fire, which affects any modern claim of mastering from them. A "stereo" copy may be the real 1958 stereo pressing or a later electronic reprocessing, and the two sound nothing alike. The mono original is the safe choice, and the reprocessed copies are worth avoiding.

- **Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Argo LP-628](https://www.discogs.com/release/371461), the 1958 mono original on black labels with a deep groove.
- **Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** The [Analogue Productions 200g](https://www.discogs.com/release/15175594) (2020), with the mastering-credit caveat set out here.
- **Best-value modern:** A Chess/Sugar Hill reissue from 1984, catalogue CH-91081.

## Recording history and tape provenance

Every set played that night was recorded, 43 tracks in all, from which Jamal chose eight. Malcolm Chisholm engineered, a credit that appears on the original release.

Sources disagree on the producer. Discogs credits Dave Usher. MusicBrainz credits Phil Chess of Chess Records, Argo's parent label. Both were plausibly involved, and neither is asserted here.

A stereo edition also appeared in 1958, cut from a multi-channel tape rather than reprocessed. Argo issued it as LPS-628.

A detailed Discogs owner account reports that the original master tapes were later lost in a fire. If that is right, Chess-era repressings made while the tapes existed are closer to the source than anything cut afterwards. Any modern claim of mastering from the original tapes needs scrutiny.

"Poinciana," the closing track, became Jamal's signature piece. Miles Davis cited Jamal's rhythmic and spatial approach as an influence on his own. A second volume, *At the Pershing, Vol. 2* (Argo LP-667), followed in 1961 from the same engagement.

## Mono, real stereo and reprocessed stereo

At least one stereo-labelled pressing is credited on its own sleeve as electronically reprocessed for stereo, by named engineers Dick Allen and Ron Mallow. That is a different record from the 1958 multi-channel stereo release.

[Kenny's Audiophile Record Reviews](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=38l7hV2EW8o) compared the reprocessed stereo against the mono directly. The reviewer describes the reprocessed pressing as a diffuse ambiance rather than real imaging. It cannot place piano, bass and drums at distinct points, and he reports listening fatigue.

The [same reviewer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=38l7hV2EW8o) describes the mono as sitting in the front row at the club. He calls it a fabulous mono recording and recommends it over the reprocessed stereo.

The practical rule is to check what a "stereo" copy actually is. The 1958 LPS-628 is a real stereo tape. Later reprocessed copies say so on the sleeve.

## Original Argo pressings (1958)

[Argo LP-628](https://www.discogs.com/release/371461) on black labels with a deep groove is the first pressing. Discogs documentation separates two mono label variants. Black label copies are earliest, identified partly by an offset trip-changer spiral in the dead wax. Grey label copies are a second pressing and the most common.

The genuine stereo LPS-628 has a dark blue label. It should not be confused with the reprocessed pressings.

This is a working-club live tape from 1958, not a studio session. No pressing of it, original or reissue, is an audiophile showcase.

## Analogue Productions 200g (2020)

Retailer copy for this reissue states it was mastered at Sterling Sound from the original tapes. The record's own runouts do not support that.

Its [Discogs entry](https://www.discogs.com/release/15175594) reports pre-existing metalwork from a lacquer cut by Trutone Mastering Labs in December 1983. The cut is credited to Carl Rowatti, with "CR 12/83" inscribed in the runout. A separate owner raised the same doubt from the other direction, finding no engineer initials matching any known Sterling Sound engineer.

Given the reported fire, 1983 metalwork may be the only route to a real connection with the source. That makes the marketing claim more misleading, not less.

None of this means the record sounds bad. Owner reviews describe it as flat and quiet, a legitimate pressing of a modest source. It is plated and pressed at Quality Record Pressings in a Stoughton tip-on jacket, catalogue APJ128.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$:** A Chess/Sugar Hill reissue from 1984, catalogue CH-91081, on blue and silver Chess labels.

**$$$:** The [Analogue Productions 200g](https://www.discogs.com/release/15175594), solid production values, with the Sterling Sound claim treated with scepticism.

**$$$$:** A clean [Argo LP-628](https://www.discogs.com/release/371461) mono original on black labels.
