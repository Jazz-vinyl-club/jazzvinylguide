# Page One on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Page One* was recorded on June 3, 1963 at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey, produced by Alfred Lion. It is Joe Henderson's debut as a leader, built around a frontline with Kenny Dorham rather than a backing band. McCoy Tyner plays piano, Butch Warren bass and Pete La Roca drums. Blue Note released it in the autumn of 1963 as BLP 4140 in mono and BST 84140 in stereo.

Three modern reissues compete here, all well regarded. The 2009 Analogue Productions 45rpm, the 2014 Music Matters 180g and the 2021 Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition. Two share a mastering engineer, and a volume-matched comparison found those two hard to tell apart. That bears directly on what the price gap buys.

- **Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Blue Note BLP 4140](https://www.discogs.com/release/3382197), the 1963 mono original.
- **Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** Close between the [Analogue Productions 45rpm](https://www.discogs.com/release/4714314) and the [Music Matters 180g](https://www.discogs.com/release/5851213).
- **Best-value modern:** The [Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/17366254-Joe-Henderson-Page-One), all-analog, mastered by Kevin Gray and pressed at Optimal in Germany.

## Recording history and tape provenance

Tyner was signed to Impulse! at the time, so the original cover credits every musician by name except him. He appears as "ETC."

Dorham wrote the two opening tracks, "Blue Bossa" and "La Mesha," and Henderson the other four, including "Recorda-Me." Both "Blue Bossa" and "Recorda-Me" became standards. Dorham had encountered bossa nova rhythms on a visit to the Rio de Janeiro Jazz Festival two years earlier.

One oddity sits in the tape rather than in any pressing. Collectors have documented that "Homestretch," closing side one, appears to come from a different mix. Its saxophone and trumpet channels are reversed against every other track. Session documentation also disagrees about whether two takes exist with different personnel.

## Identifying a first pressing

The standard reference is the *Blue Note Records Guide for Identifying Original Pressings*, compiled by Frederick Cohen of the Jazz Record Center. A first-pressing [BLP 4140](https://www.discogs.com/release/3382197) has a "NEW YORK USA" address on both labels and no deep groove. The back cover address reads "47 West 63rd St., New York 23." The dead wax carries a hand-etched Plastylite "ear," and the front cover is laminated.

Copies without the Plastylite ear postdate Liberty's purchase of Blue Note and are reissues, whatever the label says.

[LondonJazzCollector](https://londonjazzcollector.wordpress.com/2020/03/24/joe-henderson-page-one-1963-blue-note/) adds that the inner sleeve style changed about eighteen months after release, so a first pressing should not carry the later sleeve. Sleeves are easily swapped, so that proves nothing on its own.

The same writeup makes a broader point. Each detail on a checklist can be true while the overall claim of originality stays unverifiable. Treat these lists as a way to rule out obvious reissues rather than as proof.

## Original Blue Note pressings (1963)

Owner reviews describe the original recording as unusually present and well separated, with Dorham's trumpet and Henderson's tenor in distinct spatial positions rather than blended.

A [1984 Blue Note reissue](https://www.discogs.com/release/2981475-Joe-Henderson-Page-One) carries stamped BNST-84140 runouts. It is a cheap way into the stereo mix, with no published comparison against the original.

## Modern reissues

The [Analogue Productions 45rpm](https://www.discogs.com/release/4714314) (2009) came out in The Blue Note Reissues series, manufactured by EMI Music Special Markets. One detailed owner review argues it captures the sound expected of a mint Blue Note original, which is a high bar to set.

The [Music Matters 180g](https://www.discogs.com/release/5851213) (2014) was mastered by Kevin Gray at Cohearent Audio, confirmed by the runout etching. Its edition size is given as 2,500 copies on Discogs and 3,500 by one retailer, and that discrepancy is unresolved. Owner reviews describe a deep black background and thick trumpet and tenor tone.

The [Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/17366254-Joe-Henderson-Page-One) (2021) is all-analog, mastered by Kevin Gray from the original tapes and pressed at Optimal, per [Blue Note](https://store.bluenote.com/products/joe-henderson-page-one-lp-blue-note-classic-vinyl-edition). Owner reviews call it full, warm and expansive.

One owner of the Classic Vinyl edition reports the horns clipping on louder passages on his system, while rating the record highly otherwise. Another owner who compared Music Matters against Classic Vinyl found the Music Matters cut more three-dimensional.

## A volume-matched comparison

[The 'In' Groove](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xES9Wdojec8), a Phoenix record shop, compared this title's Music Matters and Classic Vinyl editions with a decibel meter. Levels were matched first, so a louder cut could not win by default.

He found the two nearly indistinguishable once matched. His reasoning is that both come from Kevin Gray working from the same tapes. An engineer with over a hundred Blue Note credits has no reason to cut the wider-release series worse.

He puts the price gap down to packaging and scarcity. Music Matters copies often resell for $200 to $300 against roughly $25 to $35 for a Classic Vinyl copy. The gatefold, thicker tip-on jacket and session photography are real, but not audio.

He also reports his Classic Vinyl copy arrived in a plain paper inner sleeve rather than the poly-lined one his other copies came with. It had light scuffs, inaudible on playback. That is one account, not a verdict on the series.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$$:** The [Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/17366254-Joe-Henderson-Page-One), close to the Music Matters cut in a volume-matched comparison.

**$$$$:** A clean [Blue Note BLP 4140](https://www.discogs.com/release/3382197) original, checked against the first-pressing details.

**$$$$$:** The [Music Matters 180g](https://www.discogs.com/release/5851213) or the [Analogue Productions 45rpm](https://www.discogs.com/release/4714314). The premium buys packaging and scarcity more than a confirmed sonic edge.
