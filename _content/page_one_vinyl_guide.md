# Page One on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Page One* was recorded June 3, 1963 at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs, NJ, produced by Alfred Lion. It's Joe Henderson's debut as a leader, and Blue Note built the session around a genuine frontline partnership rather than a backing band for a new signee: trumpeter Kenny Dorham, already an established Blue Note artist, plays alongside Henderson as a true co-lead voice, with McCoy Tyner on piano, Butch Warren on bass, and Pete La Roca on drums. Tyner's presence carries its own small piece of label politics: because he was signed to rival Impulse! Records at the time, the original cover credits every musician by name except him, listing him only as "ETC."

Dorham contributes two compositions that open the album, "Blue Bossa" and "La Mesha," while Henderson wrote the remaining four tracks, including "Recorda-Me." Both "Blue Bossa" and "Recorda-Me" went on to become genuine jazz standards, covered constantly in the decades since – "Blue Bossa" in particular, a hard bop tune inflected with the bossa nova rhythms Dorham had encountered on a visit to the Rio de Janeiro Jazz Festival two years earlier, remains one of the most commonly played tunes at jazz jam sessions worldwide. Blue Note released the album in mid-October or November 1963 (sources give slightly different months) as BLP 4140 (mono) and BST 84140 (stereo). One technical quirk worth knowing about before chasing a specific mix on this record: collectors have documented that "Homestretch," the closing track on side one, appears to come from a different mix than the rest of the album – the saxophone and trumpet channels are reversed relative to every other track, and there's some additional confusion in session documentation about whether two different takes (numbered 22 and 25) exist with different personnel. It's a genuine, documented oddity in the tape, not something specific to any one pressing.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Blue Note BLP 4140](https://www.discogs.com/release/3382197) – 1963 original mono pressing; see the note below on identifying a genuine first pressing.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** contested between two well-regarded audiophile reissues – [Analogue Productions' 2009 45rpm edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/4714314) and [Music Matters' 2014 180g edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/5851213) – see the note below.

**Best-value modern:** the Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition – all-analog, mastered by Kevin Gray, pressed at Optimal in Germany; no standalone Discogs release ID confirmed in this pass.

## Identifying a genuine first pressing, and two competing audiophile reissues

Blue Note mono originals from this era are notoriously easy to mistake for later pressings sharing the same catalog number, and this title is no exception. Per the standard collector reference (the "Blue Note Records Guide for Identifying Original Pressings," compiled by Frederick Cohen of the Jazz Record Center), a genuine BLP 4140 first pressing shows: a "NEW YORK USA" address on both labels rather than a later West Coast address; no "deep groove" ring stamped into the label paper; the back cover address reading "47 West 63rd St., New York 23"; a hand-etched Plastylite "ear" mark (a small pretzel-shaped "P") in the dead wax, indicating the original pressing plant; and a laminated front cover. Copies without the Plastylite ear mark all postdate Liberty Records' purchase of Blue Note and are reissues rather than true firsts, regardless of what the label itself claims.

Two separate, well-regarded modern audiophile reissues compete for the title of best-sounding modern version, and this guide isn't picking a winner between them since both have strong independent reputations. Analogue Productions issued a 45rpm edition in 2009 as part of "The Blue Note Reissues" series, manufactured by EMI Music Special Markets; one Discogs owner review calls it "top notch," specifically noting it holds its own against Music Matters' work. Music Matters itself issued a 180-gram, single-speed 33⅓ edition in 2014, limited to either 2,500 copies (per the matrix-confirmed Discogs release listing) or 3,500 copies (per one retailer's product description) – a real discrepancy between sources that I wasn't able to resolve, so both figures are noted here rather than one asserted as fact. It's mastered by Kevin Gray at Cohearent Audio, matrix-confirmed via the runout etching itself.

## Sound quality by pressing

### Blue Note BLP 4140 (1963 original)
The true first pressing, identifiable by the checklist above. Owner reviews describe the original recording as remarkably present and well-separated even by later Blue Note standards, with Van Gelder's engineering giving Dorham's trumpet and Henderson's tenor genuinely distinct spatial placement rather than a blended horn section sound.

### Analogue Productions 45rpm (2009)
Part of Analogue Productions' original run of Blue Note reissues, before the label's later Tone Poet and Blue Note Classic Vinyl series existed. Owner reviews single it out favorably even against Music Matters' well-regarded catalog.

### Music Matters (2014, 180g)
Matrix-confirmed mastering by Kevin Gray at Cohearent Audio. Owner reviews describe a "deep black background" with strong instrument separation, praising the thickness and body of the trumpet and tenor tones specifically.

### Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition
A more recent, more accessible all-analog reissue, mastered by Kevin Gray from the original master tapes and pressed at Optimal in Germany – a different pressing plant from Music Matters' RTI pressings, and a more affordable way to get Kevin Gray's mastering without the limited-edition pricing of the 2014 Music Matters release.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$$:** The Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition – all-analog Kevin Gray mastering at a more accessible price than the limited-edition audiophile pressings.

**$$$$:** A clean [Blue Note BLP 4140](https://www.discogs.com/release/3382197) original – verify the full first-pressing checklist above before paying a premium.

**$$$$$:** [Music Matters' 2014 180g edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/5851213), or [Analogue Productions' 2009 45rpm edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/4714314) – both are strongly regarded, genuinely contested territory rather than one clear winner.
