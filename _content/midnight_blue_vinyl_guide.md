# Midnight Blue on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Midnight Blue* was recorded January 8, 1963 at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs, NJ, produced by Alfred Lion. It's the culmination of guitarist Kenny Burrell's seven-year run on Blue Note that began in 1956, and widely considered his masterwork for the label: a deep, unhurried blues session built around Stanley Turrentine's tenor sax, Major Holley Jr.'s bass, Bill English's drums, and Ray Barretto's congas, which give several tracks a subtle Latin undercurrent beneath the blues foundation. Burrell wrote most of the material, including "Chitlins Con Carne," "Midnight Blue," "Saturday Night Blues," and the unaccompanied solo guitar piece "Soul Lament." Blue Note released it in early May 1963 as BLP 4123 (mono) and BST 84123 (stereo).

The album's influence runs well beyond jazz circles. Stevie Ray Vaughan covered "Chitlins Con Carne" on his posthumous album *The Sky Is Crying*, explicitly as a tribute to one of his musical heroes; Jimi Hendrix was reportedly a fan; the central riff bears an audible resemblance to Santana's "Black Magic Woman," probably not coincidentally; and Elvis Costello's *Almost Blue* borrowed its cover concept as a direct visual homage. Perhaps strangest of all: Hugh Romney, the counterculture figure who became Woodstock's on-stage announcer, took his stage name "Wavy Gravy" directly from Burrell's tune of the same name on this record.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Blue Note BLP 4123 mono](https://www.discogs.com/release/1208022) or [BST 84123 stereo](https://www.discogs.com/release/37618128) – see the note below on identifying genuine first pressings of each.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** genuinely contested between two respected reissues – Analogue Productions' 2008 45rpm edition and Music Matters' later 33⅓ edition – see the note below on a detailed reviewer comparison that found a surprising result.

**Best-value modern:** the Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition – all-analog, mastered by Kevin Gray, pressed at Optimal in Germany.

## A genuine surprise: the newer 33⅓ reissue reportedly beats the 45rpm

Conventional audiophile wisdom holds that a 45rpm cut, with its wider groove spacing, should outperform a standard 33⅓ pressing of the same mastering chain. This title provides a well-documented counterexample worth knowing about before assuming 45rpm automatically means better sound. Analogue Productions issued a 2×45rpm edition in 2008 (catalog AP-84123), mastered by Kevin Gray and Steve Hoffman at AcousTech Mastering and pressed at Quality Record Pressings on 200-gram vinyl – reviewed at the time as probably the definitive pressing of the album. Music Matters later issued its own 33⅓ edition, also cut by Kevin Gray but at his newer Cohearent Mastering facility, using upgraded tape playback electronics and cabling installed after the AP 45rpm session. AnalogPlanet's detailed side-by-side comparison of the two found the newer 33⅓ cut sounded "more vibrant, more 'live', faster, more dynamic, more three-dimensional" than the earlier 45rpm edition – attributing the difference not to the RPM at all, but to the substantial equipment upgrades Gray had made to his own mastering chain between the two sessions. That's one detailed, credible comparison rather than universal consensus, but it's a genuinely useful data point before assuming the 45rpm edition is automatically the safer or better choice.

## Identifying genuine first pressings

Mono and stereo first pressings of this title have documented, checkable details, per collector reference sources including Fred Cohen's Blue Note pressing identification guide. A genuine mono BLP 4123 first pressing has deep groove rings on both sides of the label, a "NEW YORK USA" address, the Plastylite "ear" mark in the dead wax alongside a Van Gelder stamp, and – a detail specific to this catalog number – the catalog number in the dead wax is stamped rather than hand-etched, unlike some other Blue Note titles from the same era. A genuine first stereo pressing (BST 84123) differs in one notable way: deep groove appears on the A-side label only, not both sides, and the catalog number is rendered slightly differently across the labels, front cover/spine, and back cover (BST 84123, 84123, and ST-84123 respectively).

## Sound quality by pressing

### Blue Note BLP 4123 (mono) / BST 84123 (stereo), 1963 original
The true first pressings, identifiable by the checklists above. One LondonJazzCollector commenter specifically praises Van Gelder's stereo mix on this title as "spot on" for the era's engineering intentions, despite stereo mixes from this period sometimes reading as unusually hard-panned by modern standards.

### Analogue Productions 45rpm (2008)
Mastered by Kevin Gray and Steve Hoffman at AcousTech Mastering, pressed on 200-gram vinyl at Quality Record Pressings. Widely praised on release and still well-regarded, though see the note above on Music Matters' later comparison. One recurring, minor owner complaint across multiple Analogue Productions titles from this era: some copies have an off-center spindle hole causing slight visible wobble, though reviewers differ on whether it's audible.

### Music Matters (33⅓)
Cut by Kevin Gray at his then-newly renovated Cohearent Mastering facility, with upgraded playback electronics and cabling relative to his earlier AcousTech setup. Reviewed by AnalogPlanet as a genuine sonic improvement over the earlier AP 45rpm edition, in a rare case of a 33⅓ reissue reportedly outperforming the 45rpm version of the same recording.

### Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition
Part of Blue Note's ongoing Classic Vinyl Reissue Series, a continuation of the label's 2019 "Blue Note 80" anniversary series, running alongside the more elaborate Tone Poet line (which has not, as of this research, treated this specific title). All-analog, mastered by Kevin Gray from the original master tapes, pressed at Optimal in Germany – a more affordable way to get Gray's mastering without limited-edition pricing.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$$:** The Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition – all-analog Kevin Gray mastering at standard pricing.

**$$$$:** A clean [Blue Note BLP 4123 mono](https://www.discogs.com/release/1208022) or [BST 84123 stereo](https://www.discogs.com/release/37618128) original – verify the pressing details above before paying a premium.

**$$$$$:** The Music Matters 33⅓ edition or the Analogue Productions 2008 45rpm edition – genuinely contested territory, see the note above before choosing.
