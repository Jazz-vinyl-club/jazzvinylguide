# A Night In Tunisia (1961, Blue Note) on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## A note on the title before anything else

Two entirely different, unrelated Art Blakey recordings share the title "A Night in Tunisia." This guide covers only the 1960-recorded, 1961-released Blue Note album (BLP 4049 / BST 84049), featuring the Lee Morgan–Wayne Shorter–Bobby Timmons–Jymie Merritt lineup of the Jazz Messengers. A separate, earlier Art Blakey's Jazz Messengers recording of the same title exists on the Vik label (Vik LX-1115, from the late 1950s), with different personnel and a completely different session history. If you're searching for a copy, double-check the catalog number and label before buying – "Vik" and "Blue Note" pressings of "A Night in Tunisia" are not the same record.

## Summary

The Blue Note *A Night In Tunisia* was recorded across two sessions, August 7 and 14, 1960, at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs, NJ, produced by Alfred Lion. It documents one of the most celebrated lineups the Jazz Messengers ever had: Art Blakey on drums, Lee Morgan on trumpet, Wayne Shorter on tenor sax, Bobby Timmons on piano, and Jymie Merritt on bass – a group that had just coalesced earlier that year on the album *The Big Beat*. Wayne Shorter's entry into the band has its own well-documented origin story: at a gig where regular tenor player Hank Mobley didn't show, Lee Morgan crossed a racetrack at a Canadian jazz festival to ask Shorter if he wanted to sit in, and Blakey's only question before bringing him aboard was reportedly "Do you have eyes?"

The album opens with an 11-minute version of Dizzy Gillespie's title composition, a staple of the band's live sets by this point, and one widely regarded as among the finest recordings of the tune ever committed to tape – Blakey's playing throughout, and especially on the closing "Kozo's Waltz," was singled out by the contemporaneous DownBeat review as a "remarkable demonstration of modern drumming." The rest of the five-song set showcases newly written originals by the band's younger members: Shorter's "Sincerely Diana" (written for Blakey's wife), Timmons's "So Tired," and two Lee Morgan compositions, "Yama" and "Kozo's Waltz." Blue Note released the album in May 1961 as BLP 4049 (mono) and BST 84049 (stereo). A companion album from the same August 1960 sessions, *Like Someone in Love*, wasn't released until 1967.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Blue Note BLP 4049](https://www.discogs.com/release/2277717) – 1961 original mono pressing; see the note below on identifying a genuine first pressing.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** [Music Matters' 2008 45rpm edition](https://www.discogs.com/release/2360049) – mastered by Kevin Gray and Steve Hoffman at AcousTech Mastering, cut at 45rpm for improved sound.

**Best-value modern:** the Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition – all-analog, mastered by Kevin Gray, pressed at Optimal in Germany; see the note below on a documented pressing-defect risk before buying.

## Identifying a genuine first pressing, and a defect risk on the modern reissue

A genuine BLP 4049 first pressing has, per standard Blue Note collector references: a laminated front jacket; "Blue Note Records Inc." text with a "47 West 63rd" New York City address and the registration mark (®) below the "E" in "NOTE" on deep-grooved labels; the Van Gelder stamp and Plastylite "ear" symbol in the dead wax; and a back-cover address reading "43 West 61st St., New York 23." One collector has also noted what sounds like a genuine tape-level anomaly rather than a pressing fault – a brief right-channel issue audible on one track that isn't present elsewhere on the record, confirmed by multiple owners rather than a single mistracking stylus.

The Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition, while well-regarded overall, has at least one documented owner report of "non-fill" – a real vinyl manufacturing defect where the pressing doesn't completely fill the stamper mold, causing audio dropouts – along with a general note that the sound quality on that particular copy wasn't up to the standard of most other Blue Note Classic releases. This appears to be an inconsistent, copy-specific issue rather than a defect affecting the entire pressing run, based on other owners reporting excellent sound and clarity from the same edition, but it's worth inspecting a copy or buying from a seller with a clear return policy.

## Sound quality by pressing

### Blue Note BLP 4049 (1961 original)
The true first pressing, identifiable by the checklist above. Given the record's stature within the Jazz Messengers' catalog, clean original copies command real collector premiums – confirm every detail of the checklist rather than assuming a period-correct catalog number alone guarantees a genuine first pressing.

### Music Matters 45rpm (2008)
Mastered by Kevin Gray and Steve Hoffman at AcousTech Mastering from the original Rudy Van Gelder tapes, cut at 45rpm across four sides, with hand-etched runouts (matrix MMBST-4049-(45)-A through D). Music Matters' Blue Note reissues from this era are consistently well-regarded, and this title is no exception.

### Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition
All-analog, mastered by Kevin Gray from the original master tapes, pressed at Optimal in Germany. A more accessible modern option than the limited-edition Music Matters pressing, though see the note above on one documented non-fill report before assuming every copy is flawless.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$$:** The Blue Note Classic Vinyl Edition – inspect the copy or buy from a seller with a clear return policy given the documented defect report above.

**$$$$:** A clean [Blue Note BLP 4049](https://www.discogs.com/release/2277717) original – verify the full first-pressing checklist before paying a premium.
