# Everybody Digs Bill Evans on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Everybody Digs Bill Evans* was recorded December 15, 1958 at Reeves Sound Studio in New York City, produced by Orrin Keepnews, with Sam Jones on bass and Philly Joe Jones on drums. Released in early 1959 as Riverside RLP 12-291 (mono) and RLP 1129 (stereo), this was Evans's second album as a leader and the one that established him as a major voice, including three unaccompanied solo piano tracks ("Lucky to Be Me," "Peace Piece," "Epilogue"). The cover carries tribute quotes from Miles Davis, George Shearing, Ahmad Jamal, and Cannonball Adderley.

This is a small-group piano trio date with a genuinely strong original recording. Mono is generally considered the more cohesive listen for this particular album, which is part of why it's had two distinct modern mono reissues. One caveat worth knowing before blaming a specific pressing: a detailed album review (Ten-Minute Record Reviews, "Bill Evans - Everybody Digs Bill Evans (Episode 434)") notes a fair amount of distortion on the piano at points on the original 1958 source tape itself – not severe enough to seriously detract from the listening experience, but present across pressings rather than a defect specific to any one of them.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** [Riverside RLP 12-291 mono / RLP 1129 stereo](https://www.discogs.com/sell/release/3416397) – 1959 first pressing, deep groove, black "twin reels and microphone" label.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** Analogue Productions AJAZ 1129-45 – matrix runouts confirm mastering by Kevin Gray (and, on some sides, Steve Hoffman) at AcousTech Mastering; no standalone Discogs release page was found, see note below.

**Best-value modern:** [Original Jazz Classics OJC-068 (1983)](https://www.discogs.com/release/1324258) – mastered by George Horn, a straightforward, honest reissue.

## A note on two disputed/unconfirmed details

The Craft Recordings CR00751 (2024 RSD mono reissue) is sold everywhere with promotional copy crediting Kevin Gray for cutting the lacquers from the original master tapes. However, Discogs' own release page for this exact pressing carries a contributor note stating that credit was incorrectly printed on the hype sticker. I could not independently confirm who actually mastered it, so the mastering attribution below is marked unresolved rather than fact.

Separately, I was not able to find a standalone Discogs `/release/` page for the Analogue Productions 45rpm pressing (AJAZ 1129-45) – only a box-set compilation listing that happens to include its matrix data. The Discogs column for that row links to the general Acoustic Sounds product listing instead.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$ (Affordable):** The [**Original Jazz Classics OJC-068**](https://www.discogs.com/release/1324258) (1983) – a solid, low-surface-noise all-analog remaster.

**$$$ (Mid-range):** The [**Craft Recordings CR00751**](https://www.discogs.com/release/30408008) 2024 mono RSD reissue – dead quiet, well-centered, cohesive mono presentation (mastering credit unconfirmed, see note above).

**$$$$–$$$$$ (Premium):** A clean [**Riverside RLP 12-291/1129**](https://www.discogs.com/sell/release/3416397) original, condition-dependent, or the [**Analogue Productions AJAZ 1129-45**](https://store.acousticsounds.com/d/12963/Bill_Evans_Trio-Everybody_Digs_Bill_Evans-45_RPM_Vinyl_Record) 45rpm reissue – owner reports single this out as the best-sounding version available.
