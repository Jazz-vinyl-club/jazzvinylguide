# Ascension on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Ascension* was recorded on June 28, 1965 at Rudy Van Gelder's studio in Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey, produced by Bob Thiele. Coltrane leads an eleven-piece ensemble rather than his usual quartet, working from sheets of chords and scales rather than fixed parts. Impulse! released it in February 1966 as A-95 in mono and AS-95 in stereo.

One fact governs buying this record. Thiele recorded two complete takes of the same 40-minute piece. Impulse! released the second, then switched to the first without changing the catalogue number, the cover or anything visible from outside the sleeve. The two takes sound different, and only the dead wax tells them apart.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** An original Impulse! A-95 or AS-95, in either edition, chosen by which take is wanted.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** The [Verve Vault Series 2LP](https://store.ververecords.com/products/john-coltrane-ascension-edition-i-ii-verve-vault-series-lp) (2026), the first vinyl release with both takes.

**Best-value modern:** A 1970s or 1980s Impulse!, ABC or MCA repress, which carries the Edition II take.

## Edition I and Edition II

Impulse! first released the second take, in February 1966. Those original pressings are now called Edition I.

Coltrane then said he preferred the first take. Impulse! swapped the metal stampers for ones cut from that earlier take and etched "EDITION II" into the dead wax. The catalogue number, cover and labels did not change, and pressing of the originally released take stopped.

The takes differ audibly. The solo order is not the same. Edition I runs about 38:30 with an Elvin Jones drum solo on side two, and Edition II runs about 40:49 without it.

On mid-to-late 1960s orange and black label copies, checking the dead wax for "EDITION II" is the quickest test. Collector reports say US pressings from the 1970s and early 1980s carry the Edition II take but stopped including the etching. A later copy without the etching is therefore not necessarily Edition I.

## Recording history and tape provenance

The ensemble has Freddie Hubbard and Dewey Johnson on trumpets. Pharoah Sanders, Marion Brown, John Tchicai and Archie Shepp play saxophones alongside Coltrane. McCoy Tyner is on piano, Art Davis and Jimmy Garrison both on bass, and Elvin Jones on drums.

Coltrane made this turn at the commercial and critical peak of his career, about six months after *A Love Supreme*. He assembled a large, largely unrehearsed group and directed the shifts between full-ensemble passages and solo spotlights mostly by gesture in the room.

His biographer Ben Ratliff described the result as an experiment in the democratic ideal. Garrison and Jones can be heard intermittently trying to anchor the piece to a conventional pulse underneath the collective improvisation, not always successfully.

## The 2026 Verve Vault Series edition

Impulse! issued *Ascension: Edition I & II* as a double LP in 2026, as part of the Coltrane Estate's centennial campaign. It is the first vinyl release to carry both complete takes in one package.

Ryan K. Smith mastered it from the original stereo analog tapes, pressed on 180g, per the [Verve store listing](https://store.ververecords.com/products/john-coltrane-ascension-edition-i-ii-verve-vault-series-lp).

No original edition held both takes. This is a different proposition from a vintage pressing, not a competing cut of the same material.

## Sound quality by pressing

Edition I is the take Impulse! chose first, with the Elvin Jones drum solo on side two and a different solo order. Genuine Edition I copies have no "EDITION II" text in the dead wax.

Edition II is Coltrane's preferred take and became the standard from 1966 onwards. Most surviving vintage copies of this catalogue number carry it rather than the originally released take.

A Japanese pressing of Edition II from Nippon Columbia in 1976 is documented, distinct from an earlier Japanese first pressing. No published comparison of the Japanese and US pressings has been found.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$:** A 1970s or 1980s Impulse!, ABC or MCA repress, which carries the Edition II take.

**$$$:** The [2026 Verve Vault Series 2LP](https://store.ververecords.com/products/john-coltrane-ascension-edition-i-ii-verve-vault-series-lp), the only release with both takes.
