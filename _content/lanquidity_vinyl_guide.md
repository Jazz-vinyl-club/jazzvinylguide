# Lanquidity on Vinyl: The Definitive Pressing Guide

## Summary

*Lanquidity* was recorded overnight on July 17, 1978 at Bob Blank's Blank Tapes studio in New York City, engineered by Bob Blank and Joe Arlotta, produced by Sun Ra himself – reportedly the same night the Arkestra had appeared on *Saturday Night Live*. Released August 14, 1978 on the small Philadelphia label Philly Jazz, catalog PJ-1006.

**Best early pressing for audiophiles:** an original 1978 Philly Jazz pressing – genuinely rare, and worth verifying carefully given the packaging variants described below.

**Best modern pressing for audiophiles:** the Strut/Art Yard 45rpm 4LP box set – remastered by Technology Works, includes both the original commercial mix and a separate, more controversial alternate mix; see below before buying.

**Best-value modern:** the Strut single-LP "definitive" edition (catalog STRUT237LP) – the widely-distributed original mix only, in more accessible packaging and pricing.

## Recording history and tape provenance

Stylistically the album is markedly different from most of Sun Ra's catalog, drawing heavily on funk, R&B, and jazz fusion rather than his more familiar free-jazz or big-band Afrofuturist material. AllMusic's review compares the title track's slow pace and elegiac tone to Charles Mingus's "Goodbye Porkpie Hat," while noting the closing track, "There Are Other Worlds (They Have Not Told You Of)," reads as closer to Ra's more familiar 1960s and early-70s style, complete with the spoken "ethnic voices" musing about outer space that recur throughout his catalog. AllMusic's own review explicitly notes the album "was extremely rare in its original vinyl pressing" – not just old-record scarcity, but a genuinely small original print run from a tiny Philadelphia label with limited distribution.

The original pressing itself has documented variation even within what's considered the first run: one detailed Discogs account, from someone who received their copy directly from Philly Jazz co-founder Tom Buchler around the time of release, describes a stark, low-budget presentation – a black-and-white photocopy pasted onto plain white sleeve stock on the front, a photocopy on yellow paper on the back, and a plain label with hand-etched runouts reading "SUN RA LANQUIDITY (P) 1978 PHILLY JAZZ INC (C) CAPITOL 9/14/78 PJ-1006-A" (and the B-side equivalent) – confirming the record was pressed at a Capitol Records manufacturing plant despite being released on a tiny independent label. The more commonly described "proper" original pressing carries the album's now-iconic metallic silver foil cover with magenta accents and silver labels with red printing; whether the photocopy-sleeve copies represent an earlier promotional batch or simply inconsistent early production at a shoestring label wasn't confirmed with certainty in this research, but both variants are documented as genuine 1978 Philly Jazz product rather than one being a bootleg of the other.

A genuinely controversial reissue element is worth understanding directly rather than glossing over. Alongside the original, widely-distributed Philly Jazz mix, a separate set of alternate mixes by Bob Blank exists – originally given a limited release in 1978 specifically for an Arkestra gig at Georgia Tech, and largely unheard by most collectors until Strut and Art Yard's later reissue programs made them widely available. LondonJazzCollector's review of the 2023 Art Yard/Strut box set doesn't mince words about this alternate version: despite what the reviewer describes as "rhapsodic reception from the music press," they personally found the alternate mixes "dense, stodgy, overdubbed for the heavy bass" compared to the original release. That's a genuine critical disagreement worth knowing about before assuming the alternate mixes are a strict upgrade just because they're presented as a special, harder-to-find bonus.

Real, documented quality-control concerns also affect at least some copies of the modern reissue program. LondonJazzCollector's own review compiles multiple Discogs owner comments describing inconsistent pressing quality: reports of warped copies, an overly tight spindle hole, and pressings described as playing "like a VG [very good, i.e. worn] when it's new" straight out of the sleeve. A separate, detailed owner review of a third modern pressing – Vinyl Me, Please's 2021 "Translucent Red" edition, mastered by Dave Cheppa, limited to 750 hand-numbered copies – directly names the source of these problems: a specific pressing identified only as "Rainbo" (referring to Rainbo Records, a well-known US pressing plant) that the reviewer calls "essentially unlistenable" due to pressing defects, contrasted against the VMP/Cheppa edition, which the same reviewer found clean, consistent, and "close to flawless." That same review also directly weighs in on the Strut/Art Yard box set discussed above, criticizing it for not taking full advantage of the 45rpm format – "45RPM on cruise control," in the reviewer's words – and preferring the VMP pressing overall, explicitly recommending it as "your go to copy for Lanquidity on wax" as of that review. This is one detailed account rather than universal consensus, but it's a specific, useful data point given how scattered and sometimes negative other reports about this title's various pressings are.

One further minor discrepancy worth flagging: at least one cataloguing source lists the original catalog number as PJ666 rather than PJ-1006. Given that PJ-1006 is directly confirmed via a hand-etched dead-wax quote from an actual original pressing (and independently referenced elsewhere), this guide treats PJ-1006 as the more reliably sourced catalog number, with PJ666 noted as a possible transcription error in that other source rather than a confirmed alternate numbering.

## Sound quality by pressing

An original 1978 Philly Jazz pressing (catalog PJ-1006) is the true first pressing – genuinely scarce given the small original run from an independent Philadelphia label, and commanding real collector prices as a result. Confirm the specific packaging variant (metallic foil cover versus the plainer photocopy-sleeve variant documented above) and hand-etched runout details before paying a premium.

The Strut/Art Yard 45rpm 4LP box set is remastered by Technology Works in London, pressed in Poland and assembled in the Czech Republic, spreading both the original commercial mix and the separate Bob Blank alternate mixes across eight sides total at 45rpm. See above on the real critical disagreement over whether the alternate mixes represent an improvement, and on documented pressing-quality inconsistencies affecting some copies.

The Strut single-LP edition (STRUT237LP) presents only the widely-distributed original Philly Jazz mix, in packaging that reproduces the original metallic foil/magenta sleeve design, at a more standard single-LP price point than the full box set.

The Vinyl Me, Please "Translucent Red" edition (2021) is mastered by Dave Cheppa, limited to 750 hand-numbered copies, on translucent red vinyl in a metallic foil cover. At least one detailed direct comparison rates this as the cleanest and most consistent modern pressing of the three covered here, ahead of both the Strut box set and copies from the "Rainbo"-pressed reissue run specifically flagged for defects.

## Buyer's guide by budget

**$$$:** The Strut single-LP edition (STRUT237LP) – the original mix only, in accessible packaging and pricing.

**$$$$:** The Strut/Art Yard 45rpm 4LP box set – includes the alternate Bob Blank mixes, though inspect for the documented pressing-quality issues noted above and know that critical opinion on the alternate mixes themselves is genuinely divided.

**$$$$$:** An original 1978 Philly Jazz pressing, if you can find and afford one – genuinely rare given the small original print run.
